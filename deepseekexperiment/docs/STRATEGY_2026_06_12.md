# Strategy verdict — best path to usable V4 Pro on the 16GB box

**Date:** 2026-06-12 (15-agent adversarial workflow + independent verification)
**Supersedes the optimistic framing in earlier roadmap docs.**

## Three corrections that reset the picture (independently verified)

1. **`route_overlap` is REF-vs-QUANT fidelity, NOT temporal locality.**
   `ppl_stack.zig:1057` compares `routing[t][0][a] == routing[t][1][b]` —
   stream 0 vs stream 1 of the *same token t*. It measures how well the
   quant model tracks the reference, full stop. Any caching/prefetch
   argument built on it is invalid. Real temporal locality is E7:
   prev-8 overlap 0.237, prev-128 0.872; LRU hit cap-32→0.158,
   cap-128→0.595, cap-256→0.857.

2. **The full P3 expert store is ~600GB and FITS ON NO DRIVE HERE.**
   26.8MB/expert × 384 × 58 = 598GB (verified). Free space: 46GB ext4 +
   74GB NTFS. Every prior tps projection assuming "P3 experts resident on
   ext4 @ 2.18GB/s" was fantasy. The experts exist only as 772GB fp4 on the
   NTFS drive @ 1.08GB/s, with a ~95ms/expert decode tax on read.

3. **The binding constraint is unbeatable by software at 16GB.**
   ~9–11GB expert fetches/token over a 1.08GB/s link = ~0.23 tps interactive,
   92% fetch stall. Compute is 8% of the token. Confirmed by E10b. No
   quantization, GPU, or routing trick moves this for single-token decode.

## The recommended path: batched offline throughput engine

Batching is the ONLY lever that moves the binding constraint: it converts
per-token random-read *latency* into once-per-step sequential *bandwidth*,
so each expert read serves B sequences. Composite chain (16GB, no hardware):

| stage | lever | effect | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | single-token decode | 0.23 tps | MEASURED |
| × | batch B sequences (expert-GEMM amortization) | ÷ by B until fetch≈compute | MECHANISM confirmed, tps PROJECTED |
| × | U3 recency substitution (ε=0.25) | 34% fetch cut + repairs ⅓ of XOR tax | CONFIRMED-LEVER |
| × | hash-layer 0–2 tid2eid prefetch | ~1.05× (free, never implemented) | CONFIRMED |
| × | shared-expert + attention RAM-pin | frees fetch budget | CONFIRMED-FREE |

**Honest projections (separated by epistemic status):**
- **MEASURED floor:** 0.23 tps interactive single-token. Does not improve. 20
  tps interactive is **physically unreachable on this silicon** — stated plainly.
- **PROJECTED, current disk (fp4/NTFS @ 1.08GB/s):** B=256→~0.36, B=512→~0.7,
  B≥853→~1.2 aggregate tps. **Honest operating point: 0.5–1.0 aggregate tps,
  short-context, offline** (~2,000–3,600 tok/hr) at the proven +0.206-nat
  intelligence.
- **PROJECTED, +1TB NVMe so P3 store goes ext4-resident @ 2.18GB/s:**
  B=512→2.0, B≥853→3.3 tps (now compute-bound). The order-of-magnitude move.

## The one load-bearing unknown → Experiment 0 (do first)

Instrument `ppl_stack.zig` to decode B=8 then B=64 independent slices of
`stream_tokens.bin` in lockstep; log per layer `|union of routed experts|`
vs B×6. **GRADUATE if** the union saturates toward ~384 at small B (each
expert read once/step, amortizes across the batch → whole composite alive).
**DIE if** union ≈ B×6 (disjoint routing → batching buys nothing → right-size
to 0.27 tps and stop). E11b's 212–369 experts/layer at B=1 predicts graduation.
Cost: ~1 day, zero hardware.

## Experiment sequence (cheapest-highest-information first)

1. **E0 batch union-saturation** (above) — the decisive premise test.
2. **E1 fix the tps model** (~10 min): replay real score-layer LRU over the
   logged E11 routing trace; `e10_tps_model.py` currently uses *hash-layer*
   Zipf locality for score layers (its notes admit it). Tells us if 0.23 is
   partly a proxy artifact.
3. **E15 functional dedup** (~5 min, QUEUED behind forge, chain 7): 384×384
   output-cosine on real inputs — the long shot (§below). Consolation prize:
   a U3 substitution-neighbor table regardless.
4. **E3 MTP** — DEFERRED until cache is warm (RAM upgrade). Net-negative on a
   cold box: adds a 62nd full MoE layer (+210MB/token guaranteed), break-even
   acceptance A≈0.79, and E11b's 67.7% top-1 on hard text is a warning.

## The long shot (E15, queued)

Whole-expert FUNCTIONAL dedup on realized inputs. U2/B8/B5 proved weight-space
incompressibility (the √(2/π) gaussian wall); nobody asked whether experts with
orthogonal row-spaces compute the *same function on the ~hundreds of inputs that
occur*. Prior says it dies (<10% siblings — the router is trained to avoid
near-duplicate routing), but 5 min, data on disk. >25% near-copies → cache
occupancy ~doubles for free.

## What to STOP doing

- **STOP the io-first recency-conveyor** — premise was the misread metric.
  Keep only hash-layer-0–2 prefetch; **kill queued U9 disk-reorg** (no-op at
  25MB granularity, bandwidth-bound not latency-bound).
- **STOP treating the GPU as a lever.** XNOR is already the fastest kernel;
  compute is 8% of the token. Shared-expert VRAM-pin cuts ZERO disk fetches
  (always-active → already resident). Wrong bottleneck. Backlog only.
- **STOP all weight-space compression** (U2/B8/B5/PQ/sparse-poly of weights) —
  dead on the gaussian wall. Redundancy is not in the weights.
- **STOP cosine/top-1 as success metric** — PPL is the only valid judge (E11).
- **STOP chasing more bit-planes** — P8 (>fp4 bits) collapsed identically; the
  "collapse" was the attention ablation (E11 recovered cos@L60 to 0.83).

## The honest ceiling

**~0.5–1.0 aggregate tps offline (short-context) on this 16GB box, no hardware.
~2–3 tps with a ~$150 used DDR4 + 1TB NVMe upgrade. Never 20 tps interactive
on this silicon.** The order-of-magnitude lever is disk+RAM, not GPU and not a
software breakthrough. The intelligence (+0.206 nats, E11b) and context (~8KB/
token, inherited) are already solved; only throughput is open, and it is
bounded by physical I/O, not cleverness.
