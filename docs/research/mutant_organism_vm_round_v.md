# Round V / V1 — mutation-complete organism VM

**Verdict: VALID NEGATIVE for representation birth; substrate mechanics pass.**

This experiment built a bounded non-language organism VM whose program, data,
layout, timing, development, reproduction, and mutation operator occupy one
64-byte mutable tape. Every tape cell was reached by the organism-encoded
mutation process, and changing the mutation-operator bytes changed descendant
distributions. The selected organism produced a small held-out gain, but that
gain is not accepted as representation birth because the VM and test worlds
share low-level byte-arithmetic biases and the gain is small and encoding
sensitive.

## What was supplied

The immutable universe supplies raw byte input, one-bit output, a 96-step energy
cap, tape bounds, freeze/copy semantics, deterministic hashing, and eight VM
effects: XOR, wrapping addition, rotation, masked raw-input mixing, tape load,
tape store, conditional relative jump, and tick mixing. It also supplies the
world/evaluator boundary and selection objective.

Those effects are a real inductive bias. “Mutation-complete” here means every
organism-owned encoded component is mutation-reachable inside this finite VM;
it does **not** mean mathematically universal, unbiased, intelligent, or able to
reach every tape efficiently.

No LLM, text, embedding, neuro-symbolic layer, named task feature, solution
Rune, imported learner, or answer key participates.

## Mutable organism

Bytes 0–7 encode executable-region start/length, storage start/length,
topological stride, scheduling period, development depth, and reproduction
period. Bytes 8–15 encode the organism's own mutation operator: target offset,
mask, rotation, repetition count, nonce mixing, and additive rewrite. Bytes
16–63 are the shared program/storage medium. VM stores can modify that medium
during an execution; reproduction mutates the frozen parent tape.

Direct perturbation changed all eight decoded control fields (`0xff`). Across
4,096 deterministic births, all **64/64** raw cells—including headers, mutation
operator, executable bytes, and storage bytes—were mutation targets.

Two parents differing only in mutation-operator bytes produced different
descendant digests (`4cf456eba1354868` vs `ea4c2a44afc185fe`) and different
numbers of changed descendant cells (**622** vs **965**) across 128 births each.
Thus mutating the mutator has an observable inherited effect rather than being
dead metadata.

## Transfer experiment

Selection saw ten development worlds. The organism was then frozen and tested
on fourteen later worlds, 80 events each, under both raw and bijectively
re-encoded inputs. Worlds are generated by a separate arithmetic recurrence and
never inspect organism bytes. All arms receive the same 409,600-event proposal
budget cap and 96 VM steps per prediction; fixed/replay arms may leave their
unused proposal allocation idle.

| Arm | Raw errors | Re-encoded errors | Total / 2,240 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| self-mutating VM | 543 | 576 | **1,119** |
| fixed | 618 | 596 | 1,214 |
| random growth | 617 | 527 | 1,144 |
| replay | 583 | 583 | 1,166 |
| equal-size static | 568 | 569 | **1,137** |
| task-aligned supplied ceiling | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The mutant wins over the equal-size static arm by only 18 errors out of 2,240
(0.80 percentage points). Its own raw-to-re-encoded shift is 33 errors. Random
growth is also better than the mutant on the re-encoded half. The task-aligned
row is an explicitly human-installed recurrence emulator ceiling, not an
autonomous learner and not evidence for the VM.

Most importantly, the “independent” recurrence still uses rotation, wrapping
addition, multiplication, and XOR over bytes, while the VM is born with closely
matched byte effects. The world does not share topology or tape, but it does
share material-level arithmetic. This cannot distinguish a born representation
from selection exploiting installed physics.

## Hostile controls and limitations

- Tape length and per-prediction energy are immutable, so size/bloat cannot buy
  the score.
- Every opcode is total under bounded indices; malformed organisms remain
  bounded and non-performing organisms receive no promotion.
- The organism has no evaluator, hidden-state, filesystem, clock, or score API.
- Held-out evaluation receives a copied frozen tape; runtime self-writes cannot
  alter the committed ancestor.
- Fresh executions produce byte-identical CSV ledgers.
- Replay has no matching held-out trajectory and does not beat the mutant.
- Bijective raw recoding exposes sensitivity rather than being averaged away.

This is still a tiny one-input/one-output finite VM. Its development and
reproduction semantics are encoded and mutable, but the meaning of the VM
effects, the transport width, selection rule, and energy physics remain human
installed. The reachability test proves coverage, not useful accessibility.

## Result used by the arc

V1 solves a narrower engineering problem: a single raw medium can safely carry
mutable code, data, organization, development, reproduction, and mutation
strategy, and mutation-strategy changes propagate measurably to descendants.

It does **not** clear the Round V representation-birth gate. The next accepted
claim needs a useful mechanism to survive worlds whose primitive physics do not
mirror the VM's installed effects, multiple unrelated raw encodings, and a
strong equal-size/equal-budget control by a margin larger than this one.

## Reproduce

```bash
zig build-exe sparse_poly_discovery/mutant_organism_vm_round_v.zig \
  --cache-dir /tmp/v1-cache --global-cache-dir /tmp/v1-global \
  -femit-bin=/tmp/mutant-vm
/tmp/mutant-vm selftest
/tmp/mutant-vm run results/mutant_organism_vm_round_v.csv
```

The self-test requires deterministic replay, all eight decoded fields and all
64 raw tape cells reachable, a real mutation-operator distribution change, and
the conservative `VALID_NEGATIVE` verdict.
