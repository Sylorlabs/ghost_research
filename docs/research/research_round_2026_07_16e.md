# Research Round 2026-07-16e (Round AH) — human-scaffold exclusion

**Status:** GATED — AH1/AH2/AH3 replayed as valid-negative infrastructure results; AH4–AH6 remain blocked.

**Search finding:** The completed AF–AG sources explicitly retain
organism-visible human scaffolds: semantic meaning and comparison
(`hostTrace`, `hostScore`, `hostClosure`, `hostResource`, `execute`,
`score`, relation builders); mutation/candidate choice (`hostProposeBoundary`,
bit/record trial generators and revision schedules); organism identity
(fixed cells/tapes/boundary tags/templates); and persistence/reproduction
(`externalLoop`, fixed seeds, host repair, viability scalar, copy/replication
scheduling). These are not merely implementation details: they determine what
the mutant can observe, change, be, and survive as.

**Goal:** Make those capabilities unavailable to the mutant, rather than
auditing them after the fact. The mutant must be only raw mutable matter inside
a uniform local interaction medium. No organism-visible call, data field,
callback, function pointer, shared memory field, source import, or process
channel may expose score, target, comparison, observation/action API, decoder,
candidate/mutation grammar, boundary/template, repair, viability, scheduler,
or reproduction routine. The fixed exterior is limited to uniform lower-level
local transport, finite conservation, sandbox/provenance, evaluator-private
post-run measurement, and authority/safety.

## Acceptance standard

The exclusion system is accepted only if it detects and blocks deliberately
seeded forbidden interfaces, keeps evaluator data/process memory inaccessible
at runtime, and permits a uniform raw medium with no hidden organism template.
This is a research infrastructure wave, not a claim that intelligence has
appeared. A raw-medium behavior result is **VALID NEGATIVE** if the firewall or
audit discovers any forbidden capability, or if a fixed-loop/template control
matches it.

## Landing protocol

Workers edit only their assigned source, CSV, and report. Every landing must
fresh-build, selftest, deterministically replay, include seeded-leak attacks,
name residual exclusions, and label **POSITIVE**, **VALID NEGATIVE**, or
**BLOCKED**. The coordinator independently replays and settles this master,
the TOC, and the index.

## Coordinator verdict (2026-07-16)

Fresh independent coordinator builds, selftests, executions, and byte-identical
CSV comparisons pass for AH1–AH3. AH1 provides a uniform capability-free
fixture with all 7 seeded forbidden requests blocked before allocation. AH3
provides a fail-closed audit manifest and blocks all 11 deliberate direct and
indirect scaffold fixtures. AH2 blocks all 15 mounted protocol-level capability
attacks and leaves no evaluator scalar/handle/callback route.

This is a valuable exclusion infrastructure result, but not true hostile
containment or an autonomy result. AH2 correctly demonstrates the decisive
residual: hostile same-process, same-UID Zig source can import standard
facilities and bypass an API/struct convention. AH3 also cannot prove arbitrary
compiled, kernel, or side-channel behavior. AH4–AH6 are therefore blocked.

Before another organism behavior wave, the firewall must become process-level:
the raw medium needs a separately launched unprivileged process or namespace,
no filesystem/network/process authority, a narrow explicit byte-stream
transport interface, and evaluator one-way post-run collection. The audit must
inspect the launch policy, process tree, syscall/namespace evidence, binary
provenance, and trace. Only then does “the mutant cannot use human parts” mean
more than “this fixture did not call them.”
